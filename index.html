Paragraph Structure Checker

Paste one paragraph to check its basic structure before you submit it. This tool checks structure only. It does not grade your ideas, and it will not rewrite your work.

What is being checked
  • Single paragraph: the text is one paragraph (complete / incomplete).
  • Three distinct quotations: at least three different direct quotations (complete / incomplete). A missing or mismatched quotation mark does not stop one from counting; it is noted so you can fix it. A paraphrase (a source's ideas in your own words) is not a quotation, even with a citation.
  • Framework before case: the theory or approach from the course comes before the case it is used to interpret (complete / incomplete).
  • Evidentiary claim: each quotation is rated Strong, Moderate or Weak on whether the quoted words themselves stand on their own as evidence: a specific claim built on a direct noun or named instance (a named policy, law, event, organisation, place, date or figure). Naming the author does not count.
  • Topic sentence: one or two opening sentences that state your main claim clearly and declaratively, in your own voice (no quotations or citations).
  • Explanation after each quotation: the sentence right after a quotation, in your own words. After a framework quotation, explain the author's idea. After a case quotation, say why the case is there and how the framework interprets it.
  • Ending: a sentence on the overall significance, then a concluding sentence that restates the topic and bridges to the next paragraph, in your own voice.
  • Declarative claims: claims stated plainly and owned. Flags claims that are implied or hedged instead of stated, negative framing, and "not X but Y" constructions (shown as "If not X, then Y").
  • Possible typos: listed as a note at the bottom.
